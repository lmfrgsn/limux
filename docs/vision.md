# Limux vision

Limux is an opinionated, cohesive desktop environment built from existing Linux components, not a distribution or a collection of unrelated dotfiles. It combines the comfort of classic Macintosh interfaces with the flexibility, speed and keyboard-driven strengths of modern Wayland. Limux is the product; Rhapsody is its first theme.

Inspired by Braun industrial design and Dieter Rams and Dietrich Lubs, including the ET 66, Limux favours functional, understated design. Every element has a purpose. Typography, alignment and spacing are precise; colour is restrained and conveys meaningful states. Recessed surfaces, defined controls and delicate borders lend subtle tactility. Consistency and modern usability matter more than decoration. This is an interpretation for a digital desktop, not a calculator replica.

Rhapsody draws on Apple's late-1990s Rhapsody, Macintosh Platinum and NeXTSTEP. It should feel calm, technical, precise, slightly nostalgic and modern. Cool lavender-grey surfaces and light system chrome accompany dark developer tools, compact comfortable type, subtle depth and clear focused, selected, inactive and disabled states. Accents are small and purposeful. Initial references are canvas #DADAE3, surface #F8F8FA, chrome #EAE9F0, accent #77768F, text #343442 and dark terminal #2F303B. IBM Plex Sans and Mono are candidates. Refine these references for contrast, hierarchy and accessibility; they are not immutable. Future themes can look entirely different while preserving functionality.

Limux owns shortcuts, Command-key behaviour, workspaces, gestures, launchers, clipboard and window management. Rhapsody owns presentation. Theme changes must never alter input, hardware or essential window-management behaviour.
